# Toys "R" Us Huntsville, Alabama — store #8809

## Headline
The 1990s Huntsville Toys "R" Us was **not** a freestanding brown-roof prototype. It was a conversion of the **west half of the 1966 Loveman's department-store anchor** at the south end of Huntsville's first enclosed mall, literally named **The Mall**, at the northwest corner of Memorial Parkway (US 231/431) and University Drive (US 72). That is why people remember it as "University Drive by the Parkway."

Address: **1001 Memorial Parkway NW, Huntsville, AL 35801** (Yelp lists "1001 Memorial Pkwy Mall #1"). Madison Square Mall is ~4 miles west on University Drive and is not adjacent.

## Timeline
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Mar 1966 | The Mall opens (425,000 sq ft); Loveman's anchor at the south end, JCPenney at the north |
| May 1980 | Loveman's closes |
| **Oct 16, 1984** | **Toys "R" Us #8809 opens in the west (rear, parking-lot side) half of the Loveman's box** |
| Apr 15, 1988 | Books-A-Million opens in the east half |
| early 1990s | Calhoun Community College ("the Mallege") uses inline mall space; the mall is mostly dead |
| ~1998 | The Mall is demolished and replaced by "The Fountain" power center; **only the Loveman's/TRU/BAM building survives** |
| late 1990s | TRU and BAM seal their doors onto the mall court; TRU entrance is the exterior door only |
| Oct 2, 2011 | Store closes; #8809 relocates to a 58,000 sq ft Toys/Babies "R" Us side-by-side at 6884 Governors West NW (Bridge Street) |
| Jun 29, 2018 | Bridge Street store closes in the national liquidation (now Main Event) |

A separate Babies "R" Us existed at 6275 University Dr NW (Westside Centre) in the 2000s. No Kids "R" Us was ever found for Huntsville.

## Building
- Current use: Planet Fitness + Dollar Tree in the TRU half; BAM half was Books-A-Million until recently.
- Square footage is **not on record**. Mall total 425k, Penney's 126k, so Loveman's was plausibly ~90–110k; the TRU half is therefore ~45–55k sq ft, matching the 46,000 sq ft corporate prototype (SEC 10-K FY1995). The model uses a 230' × 200' box.
- Loveman's was one story but framed with escalator wells for a never-built second floor, so the box is very tall (≈34' parapet in photos).
- The main customer entrance faced the parking lot on the west/rear side; a second door opened onto the mall's Loveman's court (sealed by the late 90s).
- Surroundings in the 90s: empty JCPenney to the north, a dead Alabama Theatre outparcel, a large mostly empty lot, the University Drive overpass to the south, a small "Brake Check" auto-center building (also addressed 1001) at the SE corner with a red/yellow band.

## Visual evidence (photos reviewed)
1. **Sky City blog, Jan 1998 (Evans Criswell) — mall-side entrance.** White fascia with the 1980s block-letter TOYS "R" US logo: red T, orange O, yellow Y, red-pink S, an outlined mirrored "R" in quotes, green U, blue S. To its left a sealed BOOKS A MILLION door. To the right, a wall of **vertical rainbow stripes (orange, green, blue, yellow, red) on ridged/corrugated panel** — the classic 80s stripe treatment. Through the doorway: a white interior wall with a **royal blue horizontal band** and a light tile floor. The mall floor is dark brick-red quarry tile under a copper-tinted slat ceiling.
2. **Mallmanac, 2000s — exterior TRU wall.** A blank whitewashed panelized wall with vertical joints, the 1999+ star-in-the-R logo mounted high, ghost outlines of the earlier 80s letters above it, a single steel door, red-painted curb. Mallmanac also describes the original Loveman's exterior as "aquamarine brick in a rectangular block pattern."
3. **Mallmanac — BAM canopy.** Tan split-face block pier, deep flat concrete canopy with can lights; a TRU road sign visible in the distance toward the University Drive overpass.
4. **Mallmanac — rear wall.** Painted CMU/stucco with a low block utility annex, rooftop conduit.
5. **1995 lease plan** (Mallmanac) showing TRU and BAM splitting the Loveman's block; **1970 lease plan** with the four-ball "the mall" logo; **1998 photo of the mall directory** showing the subdivided anchor.

## Local memories
- "I remember going to Toys R Us as a kid, but even then the rest of the mall was kind of a ghost town." (City-Data, 2017)
- "I remember when Books A Million and Toys R Us still had their mall entrances open." (City-Data)
- After BAM and TRU sealed their mall doors "it made the inside even spookier and more dead." (Sky City comments)
- Facebook "Huntsville Revisited" has posts titled "Last two days of Toys R Us" and "Do you remember Loveman's which eventually became Toys R Us" (login required).

## Not confirmed
- Exact square footage of the TRU half; exact exterior treatment before the whitewash; whether the store received a Concept 2000 remodel (97 stores had by Jan 1999; the star logo on the wall proves at least a sign update).
- Interior layout specifics — no interior photos of this store were found. The interior in the model follows the corporate 1990s format (see 02-store-format.md).
